Ancient rainforest in the morning. Pacific sunset by dinner.

Grays Harbor County is the gateway to the Washington Coast. The Rainforest to the Coast road trip links the mossy giants of the Quinault Rain Forest with miles of open Pacific beach, less than an hour apart, and the harbor towns in between. Everything is on one easy loop off I-5.

  • Hike among some of the largest trees on Earth
  • Dig razor clams, beachcomb and storm-watch on the coast
  • Fresh-off-the-boat seafood in Westport

Is this your kind of trip?

You want big trees and quiet trails

The Quinault Rain Forest is one of only three temperate rain forests in the Western Hemisphere, and its "Valley of the Giants" is home to the world's largest Sitka spruce.

You want room to breathe on the beach

Driftwood, dunes and surf stretch along the Northern and Southern Beaches. Fly a kite, dig clams, ride a bike on the sand or just watch the storms roll in.

You want real towns, not tourist traps

Working marinas, timber-baron mansions, murals, museums and local distilleries give every stop a story, and a good meal.

The route

Five stops, one loop, no wrong turns

Come in from I-5 on US-12, then follow US-101 and the coast highways. Drive it in a weekend or take a week.

    Quinault: the rainforest

    A glacier-carved lake ringed by mossy old-growth spruce, hemlock, fir and cedar. Drive the 30-mile loop around the lake, look for Roosevelt elk and bald eagles, and bring rain gear.

  2. 2

    Northern Beaches: laid-back coast

    Ocean Shores, Seabrook, Pacific Beach, Moclips, Copalis, Ocean City. Tide pools at Damon Point, bikes on the beach, campfires, and cozy cottages for storm-watching.

    Explore the Northern Beaches

    Southern Beaches: surf and seafood

    Westport and Grayland. Washington's largest coastal marina has charter fishing and whale watching, plus surf breaks and the Cranberry Coast's bogs, which blossom in late June and are harvested in fall.

    Explore the Southern Beaches
    Plan your stay with Experience Westport
  4. 4

    Port Cities: history and harbor

    Aberdeen, Hoquiam, Cosmopolis. Timber-baron homes, downtown murals, the 7th Street Theatre, and the Grays Harbor National Wildlife Refuge, where hundreds of thousands of shorebirds stop during migration.

    Explore the Port Cities
  5. 5

    East County: the gateway

    Elma, McCleary, Montesano. Elma is about 30 miles west of Olympia. Stretch your legs at Friends Landing or detour north of Montesano to Lake Wynoochee's falls.

    Explore East County

Pick your pace

A weekend, three days, or the full loop

Day 1 · Rainforest

Head out on US-12 and grab coffee in Aberdeen. Then go north on US-101 to Lake Quinault. Spend the afternoon on the lakeside trails and the loop drive, then have dinner by the lake.

Day 2 · Coast

Drive west to the Northern Beaches. Walk Damon Point in Ocean Shores, have lunch and browse the shops in Seabrook, then catch sunset on the sand before heading home.

Day 1 · Harbor towns

Visit the Aberdeen murals and historic homes, then the 7th Street Theatre district in Hoquiam. Walk the refuge boardwalk at Grays Harbor National Wildlife Refuge.

Day 2 · Rainforest

Spend a full day at Lake Quinault: morning hike, the loop drive with elk-spotting stops, and a canoe on the lake in summer.

Day 3 · Northern Beaches

Visit the tide pools at Damon Point, ride a bike on the beach, and dig razor clams if the season is open.

Day 1 · East County

Visit Friends Landing in Montesano or the falls at Lake Wynoochee, then have dinner in Aberdeen.

Day 2 · Port Cities

See the murals, museums and historic homes, then walk the wildlife refuge boardwalk and finish with an evening show at the 7th Street Theatre.

Day 3 · Quinault

Rainforest trails, the 30-mile loop drive, and an overnight by the lake.

Day 4 · Northern Beaches

Ocean Shores, Seabrook and Pacific Beach: beachcombing, kites, clams and a campfire.

Day 5 · Southern Beaches

Go out on a Westport charter or whale-watching trip, take in the view near the lighthouse, and drive the Cranberry Coast through Grayland.

When to go

Every season has its moment

Spring

Shorebird migration fills the Grays Harbor wildlife refuge, and the rainforest is at its greenest.

Summer

Long beach days, canoeing on Lake Quinault, festivals, and cranberry blossoms in late June.

Fall

Cranberry harvest along the Cranberry Coast, fall fishing, and quieter trails.

Winter

Storm-watching, king tides, holiday lights, and the rainforest living up to its name.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Quinault really a rainforest?

Yes. The Quinault Rain Forest is one of only three temperate coniferous rain forests in the Western Hemisphere. It sits at the southern end of Olympic National Park and Olympic National Forest.

How long does the drive take?

You can see the highlights in two days. Three to five days lets you slow down, add the Southern Beaches, and stay overnight in both the rainforest and on the coast. The itineraries above show what fits.

What should I pack?

Layers, rain gear and good walking shoes, even in summer. Bring a camera and binoculars for elk, eagles and shorebirds.

Can I dig razor clams?

Yes, when a dig is open. You'll need a Washington shellfish license, and dig dates and beaches are set by the Washington Department of Fish & Wildlife, so check their razor clam page before you go.

Do I need a pass for the trails?

Some trailheads around Lake Quinault are on national park or national forest land and may need a recreation pass. Check the trailhead signs or the park and forest websites before you go.

Is the route good for kids and dogs?

Yes. Beaches, short forest loops, tide pools and bike rentals all work for families, and many beaches welcome dogs on leash. Check each park's rules.
